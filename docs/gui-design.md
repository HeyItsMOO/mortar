# GUI design

The design system and screen rules for Mortar's UI. Read it before any UI change; the code in `frontend/src` is the record of every label, tooltip and state, so per-screen text here gives purpose, structure and the rules that are not obvious from the code. Concrete (`LethalModding/Concrete`, archived) is one reference for the look, not a template to copy.

## Window

- **Logo:** the running-bond mark, seven rounded bricks in three courses, filled in the primary colour; used in the title bar at 18px, as the app icon (and the Linux desktop icon) on a dark rounded square, and on desktop notifications.
- Frameless on a solid window ([architecture.md](architecture.md#stack)): 1280×720 by default, 768×432 minimum, as Concrete. The frame is a 1px light border with rounded corners, since GNOME draws no shadow for a frameless window.
- `user-select: none` everywhere except text fields and the console. The WebKit default context menu is suppressed except on an editable field (`input`, `textarea`, `[contenteditable="true"]`) or with a text selection.
- Icons come from one set, Lucide (MIT), at 1.5-2px stroke; no hand-drawn or mixed icons.

### Title bar

48px (40px compact), `--wails-draggable: drag` with its buttons not draggable; double-click maximises. Code: `shell/TitleBar.tsx`, `shell/AppMenu.tsx`, `shell/titleMenus.ts`. Left to right:

- **Mortar menu:** logo and name with a chevron (the label folds away when compact), a dropdown at least 280px wide that grows to its longest item. It holds settings (Ctrl+,), update checks, What's new, Downloads (Ctrl+J), the data folder, About, Help (Get help on F1 opens the Console's log upload and is off outside a game; Report a bug opens a prefilled GitHub issue whose **Attach diagnostics** box saves the redacted zip first and toasts to drag it into the issue, with **Show file**; **Source code** is the AGPL-3.0 source offer and opens `https://github.com/Rethunk-Tech/mortar`; **Join our Discord** opens the invite), a condensed Nexus line (account name or "Not signed in", **Premium** in the accent colour) with **Sign out of Nexus** or **Sign in to Nexus…** (opens Settings › Accounts), and **Quit Mortar**. Rows with a binding show its hint. Save diagnostics is in Settings › About, not here.
  - F5 and **Check mods and Mortar for updates** ask SMAPI's API again for the open profile's mods, ignoring cached answers, and check Mortar itself; one toast reports both, with **View** when a new Mortar is ready.
- **Game switcher**, after a divider: the open game's 22px art and name with a chevron, **Choose a game** when none is open, the game's name during its setup. The menu lists playable games in Game select's order (three most recent under **Recently played**, the rest by name under **Games**), a tick on the current one, each row led by 22px art; **All games…** opens Game select. First run shows a lone "Setup" label; Settings adds "/ Settings" after the switchers.
- **Profile switcher**, after a "/" while a game is open: the profile's mark (or a primary dot), name and an "N mods" chip (folds away when compact). Its menu lists the game's profiles under **<GAME> PROFILES** as "Name · N mods" (Profiles page order, tick on the open one), then **New profile…**, an **Import** group (the Profiles page's import entries), **Find a mod in all profiles…**, **Manage profiles…** and, when the game has templates, **Manage templates…**. Right-click, the Menu key or Shift+F10 on the button, and right-click on a profile row, open the shared profile menu ([Profile management](#profile-management)).
- **Right:** a **downloads pill** only while the queue has items under way (icon, "N downloading", a thin bar, the batch percentage; icon and percentage when compact; click opens the Downloads panel), the **notifications bell**, then minimise, maximise, close. With nothing under way the title bar stays quiet and Downloads is reached from the Mortar menu, the notifications footer and the command palette.
- **Notifications:** a 36×34 bell with an unread count opening a 360px solid dark panel (as tall as its content, up to 70% of the window, then scrolling) with **Clear** (off when empty). **New** holds this session's unread toasts newest first (a toast's action, such as Undo, is offered only while it still applies); **Earlier** holds read toasts, then the open profile's change history newest first: label (**Known good** for a restore point), change counts, time with clock time, **Show diff** and **Undo** (not on a trimmed event). The footer has **All changes…** (the full history dialog) and **Downloads…**. Ctrl+Shift+N opens it.

Banners and dialogs from the shell:

- **Startup repair:** "Mortar tidied up N things" appears once after launch when startup repaired something; its details say what and where.
- **Mortar update ready:** a full-width info banner under the title bar when a background self-update is staged: it applies when Mortar closes, and offers **Restart now** (same action as Settings › Updates).
- **What's new:** after an upgrade, a wide dialog once per version shows that release's GitHub notes with **Got it**: counts of new features and fixes, one section per `### Area` the notes name under New and Fixed (each with its icon), every line chipped **New**, **Changed** or **Fixed**, closing lines (glibc, full changelog) small at the end; notes without areas show New, Changed and Fixed sections; offline skips until a later start ([architecture.md](architecture.md#release)). It also opens from the Mortar menu and Settings › Updates. Code: `updates/WhatsNewDialog.tsx`.
- **Tray:** with **Keep Mortar in the tray** (Settings › General) on, closing the window keeps Mortar running and the tray icon offers Show Mortar, up to three **Play** entries for the most recently launched profiles (disabled while the game runs), a running-game status line and Quit; clicking the icon shows or raises the window; Show opens a fresh window that the desktop places as new. When a Mortar-started run ends, a desktop notification says **Game closed** or that the game crashed, with how many mods logged errors; clicking it raises the window on that profile's Console. Helper text notes that GNOME may need the AppIndicator extension. With the setting off there is no tray icon and close quits. **Start minimised** hides the window at launch; **Remember window** restores size and position, clamped to a visible screen.

### Menus and the command palette

- Menus (mod menu, shared profile menus, every other menu) use the dark paper `rgba(28,28,34,0.99)`, a 1px light border and 8px corners (`MuiMenu` in `frontend/src/theme/theme.ts`); items with an obvious icon carry a Lucide one. Every divider is the shared `MenuRule` (4px above and below); no menu hand-rolls one. One set of profile items (`ProfileMenuItems`) serves the switcher's context menu and the Profiles row ⋯ menu ([Profile management](#profile-management)).
- Tooltips on menu rows and rails use `OneTip`: one tooltip at a time, closed by a press.
- **Command palette** (`commandPalette/`): Ctrl+K opens a solid dialog with a search field over actions (Play, Open Downloads, Import, Share, New profile, Stream overlay), destinations (the game's profiles and tabs, the open profile's user mods, each Settings section) and the shortcuts that run from anywhere. Header order: **Recent** (last five picks, only while the search is empty), **Go to**, **Actions**, **Settings**, **Mods**. A shortcut that does what an action does shows its keys on that action, not a second row; shortcuts for the focused list (arrows, Space, Enter, Delete, Ctrl+A, Ctrl+F, Esc) are left out. Actions that need an open profile are greyed with "Open a profile first." in place of their hint. The list is 60% of the window tall; matching is fuzzy; focus lands in the search field; arrows, Enter and Esc work from anywhere while it is open; choosing an item closes it. SMAPI's bundled mods and the Mortar bridge are omitted, as on the Mods tab. Like other shortcuts it stays silent while a field has focus or a dialog is open; Esc always works.

### Input, focus and accessibility

- Every focusable control shows a visible focus ring when reached by keyboard: a 2px outline in the primary colour, 2px outside the control; a whole-row or whole-tile cover button draws it 2px inside, since its row clips what spills over.
- Everything works at 1280×800 (the Steam Deck) with the arrow keys, Tab and Enter that Steam Input maps a controller to, and no screen scrolls sideways. Game select tiles and Browse results move focus with the arrow keys (`shell/arrowFocus`: the nearest item in the next row for Up and Down; a Browse card's Add or Download action takes focus), and the mod list drops optional columns, the last compact-hidden ones first, until the name keeps 160px (`fitListColumns`). `e2e/deck.pw.ts` checks it.
- **Gamepad** (Gamepad API: WebKitGTK reads pads through libmanette, WebView2 natively, Steam Input's virtual pad in Game Mode): the d-pad and left stick send arrow keys to the focused control first, so lists, tabs, menus and arrow grids keep their own movement, and otherwise move focus to the nearest control, staying inside an open dialog, menu or drawer and stepping into a focusable scroll area's controls. A is Enter (then a click when nothing took it), B is Esc, LB and RB select the previous and next profile tab, Start plays the open profile; a held direction repeats after 400 ms every 120 ms. Focus the pad moves always shows the ring (`html[data-input="gamepad"]` until the next pointer press), since browsers ring script focus only after keyboard use. Code and checks: `shell/gamepad`, `e2e/gamepad.pw.ts`.
- **Touch or a gamepad as the main input** (a coarse primary pointer at startup, or a pad's first press for the rest of the session; `theme/roomy`): every control is at least 44×44px (buttons, icon buttons, tabs, menu items, checkboxes, text fields), the compact density gives way, and a resize handle keeps its thin line with an invisible 44px touch strip centred on it. Nothing else changes, so the same screens fit 1280×800. A link in a line of text keeps its size (WCAG 2.5.8's inline exception). `e2e/deck.pw.ts` checks Mods, Browse and the Downloads dialog for targets under 44px and runs axe-core on each.
- **Play prompt:** in play mode (`--play … --steam-session` from a Steam shortcut, as in Game Mode; [architecture](architecture.md#command-line)) the only thing Mortar draws before a fix is the launch dialog that blocked Play, unchanged, alone on the window background in a 560×480 window with 44px targets. `frontend/e2e/play.pw.ts` checks the prompt, Cancel, a fix and a clean run.

### Buttons and dialogs

- **Dialog actions:** Cancel or Close is always a text button, leftmost; at most one contained primary sits rightmost; a destructive primary is contained `color="error"`; other actions beside them stay text. Saving a name that exists (launch preset, config preset, template, saved group) asks **Replace <name>?**; deleting a preset says **Delete preset**.
- Button and chip labels never wrap (`white-space: nowrap`); a long message gets its own full-width row and truncates with an ellipsis rather than squeezing the buttons beside it.
- Dialogs that edit or confirm use solid paper with no open/close transition ([Surfaces and colour](#surfaces-and-colour)). Confirmation, prompt and discard-guard dialogs: `shell/ConfirmDialog.tsx`, `shell/PromptDialog.tsx`, `shell/useDiscardGuard.tsx`.

## Density

One spacing scale per **Density** level (Settings › Appearance › Display), set as CSS variables on `:root` (`theme/density.ts`, applied in `theme/theme.ts`). Layout code reads them through `space` and never hand-sets pixels for these roles, so a level change moves every page together. Roles: gutter, gap, pad, row, menuY, control and nav (a navigation item such as a Settings page link). Touch or a gamepad (`theme/roomy`) still raises every target to 44px.

| Token | Role | Comfortable | Compact |
| ----- | ---- | ----------- | ------- |
| `--m-gutter` | a page's left and right edge, a controls row's inset | 16px | 12px |
| `--m-gap` | between controls in a row; between a row and what follows | 8px | 6px |
| `--m-pad` | inside a card or panel | 16px | 12px |
| `--m-row` | a table or list row | 36px | 30px |
| `--m-menu-y` | above and below a menu item's text, and a menu's own top and bottom | 6px | 4px |
| `--m-control` | buttons, dropdowns, fields and the page-actions slot that share a row | 36px | 32px |

Fixed on purpose: spacing of 6px and under (icon-to-label gaps, chip and badge insets, a row's inner rhythm), vertical margins between a heading and its text, indents (`pl: 4`), zero padding resets, chrome that is not a layout role (window buttons, title bar), and widths such as grid column minimums and panel widths.

The tab header is `--m-control` plus twice `--m-gap` tall (52px comfortable, 44px compact).

## Surfaces and colour

- **No gradients** anywhere: not as backgrounds, scrims over art, fallbacks or placeholder art. Legibility over art comes from a solid layer with alpha; a missing image becomes a solid tone. The one exception is an image's edge fading to transparent through an alpha mask.
- **The window is solid.** Dark uses an opaque `rgb(25,25,30)` base, light an opaque `rgb(236,236,241)`. Behind the surfaces sits the wallpaper backdrop under a static tint (`--mortar-tint`): dark `rgba(25,25,30,0.8)`, light `rgba(244,245,247,0.88)`.
  - Dark surfaces keep their alpha and composite over it: background `rgba(25,25,30,0.80)`, paper `rgba(50,50,60,0.80)`, the app drawer `rgba(40,40,48,0.92)`.
  - Light surfaces are solid (`#ffffff` paper and panels, `#ececf1` chrome), with no translucent fills that turn muddy over the wallpaper.
  - Dialog paper (`MuiDialog` in `frontend/src/theme/theme.ts`) and the download queue sheet are opaque so the text they cover never shows through. Dialog and drawer backdrops are a static overlay (`--mortar-overlay-30`); dialogs and their backdrops open and close without a transition; the launch overlay uses `--mortar-overlay-80`.
  - Every popover (menus, the Saves backups list, pickers) is the solid menu surface with a hairline border, never the translucent paper colour.
- **Theme** (Settings › Appearance): Dark, Light or Follow system (`prefers-color-scheme`), stored as `theme` (`dark`, `light`, `system`). App chrome only; game and profile settings are unchanged. Dark is the lead theme: screenshots, README and store listings show it.
  - Both themes keep the chosen accent hex as a fill, with white or near-black text on it, whichever reaches 4.5:1.
  - Text and icons tinted with the accent use `--mortar-accent-ink`: the accent on dark, the normal ink on light, where pastel accents are unreadable as text.
  - Other tokens: title bar fill `--mortar-title-bar`; the drop overlay and first-run tour spotlight dim the window with `--mortar-scrim`. Hard-coded chrome colours live as `--mortar-*` tokens from `frontend/src/theme/palette.ts` so both palettes share one set of surfaces.
- **Background** (Settings › Appearance): a three-way toggle stored as `background` (and `backgroundImage`), all opaque and applied at once. Image and Desktop draw the wallpaper full-window behind everything, the title bar included, `object-fit: cover`, fully opaque under the tint; it never fades or filters. The window loads it from `/background?mode=` (`mode` equals the stored value), answered with `Cache-Control: no-store`.
  - **Image:** the chosen image, else the system Fedora wallpaper, else the bundled copy; preview with **Choose image…** (PNG, JPEG, GIF or WebP) and **Reset to default**. An animated GIF, WebP or APNG plays, served whole rather than shrunk; under Reduce motion the first frame shows.
  - **Desktop:** the user's own wallpaper, read at runtime (GNOME `org.gnome.desktop.background` `picture-uri-dark` under the dark colour scheme, else `picture-uri`; Windows the `HKCU\Control Panel\Desktop` `Wallpaper` value, measured on Windows 11 26H2); a wallpaper changed while Mortar is open shows at the next mode change. It falls back to Image's choice when unreadable.
  - **Solid:** no image, the plain base.
  - **Default wallpaper:** Fedora 44's `f44-01-night` (Fedora Design Team, CC-BY-SA-4.0, credited in About): the system copy `/usr/share/backgrounds/f44/default/f44-01-night.jxl` on Linux when it exists (WebKitGTK 2.54 decodes JPEG XL, measured), else a bundled 2560px JPEG.
- **Text:** primary `rgba(255,255,255,0.90)`, secondary `rgba(225,225,230,0.95)`. Links are secondary text with a dotted underline.
- **Status colours** from Concrete: info `#2B8BDA`, success `#0CDF64`, warning `#F3B416`, error `#C70A0A` on dark; light darkens them until they meet 4.5:1 on paper.
- **Primary colour** is the user's choice (Settings › Appearance): Sand `#D6B17A`, Moss `#93B86A`, Copper `#D98C5F`, Sky `#79AEDC`, Rose `#E08A9B`, Lavender `#A893DE`, Teal `#5DB8AE` or Slate `#94A7BC` (`frontend/src/theme/accents.ts`). It applies at once everywhere the primary appears, is never darkened, and is the same for every game; game colour appears only in hero art.
- **Scrollbars** are themed: 0.5em wide, in the primary colour at alpha 0.08 for the track, 0.45 for the thumb and 0.7 on hover.
- **One-line status alerts** (a callout above a list, a read error with Retry, a settings status line) are the theme's compact Alert (`<Alert variant="compact" severity=…>`): 38px tall like the update bar, 14px text, small action buttons, the severity's callout fill and line. Size and colour come from the variant, never from a component's own `sx`. A window-wide banner, an alert with a title, and notes inside a dialog keep the standard Alert. Callout actions order text secondaries left and one contained primary right.
- **Fix and action buttons are never blue**; severity colours (warning for real conflicts, error for destructive) carry weight, and harmless rows use outlined buttons.

## Type

Open Sans (300, 400, 500, 600, 700) from `@fontsource/open-sans`, `htmlFontSize` 18 with MUI's responsive font sizes, as Concrete. Every string goes through Lingui.

## Copy

How every string reads. One wording per message: `bun run --cwd frontend i18n:dupes` fails when two msgids differ only in case, punctuation or placeholder names.

- **Enable / Disable** a mod, plugin or option; never "Switch on/off", "Turn on/off" or "Activate".
- **Remove** takes something out of a profile, group or list and leaves the thing itself; **Delete** destroys it on disk (a profile, saved group, template, preset, backup, downloaded file). The button, its confirmation and its toast use the same verb.
- Sentence case everywhere: "Open settings", not "Open Settings". A fragment that must be lowercase is lowercased in code, so one msgid serves both.
- Labels, buttons, headings and toast titles take no full stop; a sentence or line of body text does. Change lines and disabled-source reasons start with a capital ("Added <mod>").
- One placeholder name per concept: `{name}`, `{count}`, `{profile}`, `{game}`, `{version}`, `{path}`, `{when}`, `{min}`, `{max}`. No positional `{0}` and no per-site names (`{aName}`, `{targetName}`); plurals use `{count}`.
- A trailing "…" only on a control that opens further input (a dialog, a picker); the dialog's own title has none. A control that acts at once has none, and "…" is never a loading marker inside a sentence.
- No em dashes in text; use a colon, a full stop or a comma (an empty table cell may show one). Straight quotes and apostrophes only; a name goes in a placeholder without quotes.
- Errors read "Could not <verb> <thing>", never "Couldn't", "Failed to" or "Unable to"; no "Please".
- Shortest wording that keeps every fact: what it does, and when it matters.
- **Times:** a moment within the last week reads relative ("a few seconds ago", "3 minutes ago", "yesterday", "3 days ago"); older ones show the date, with the time where the list is a timeline (runs, backups, profile history, notifications). This covers every list column, hero, card and status line that shows a time.
- **Names that cannot be resolved** read **Unknown mod** with the UniqueID in the tooltip, shown only when no id is known either.

## Game Select

Mortar opens on the last game used; Game Select shows at start only until a game has been opened, and otherwise from the title bar's Game Select tab. Code: `games/GameSelect.tsx`, `games/LetterRail.tsx`, `games/order.ts`.

One list of full-width game rows, 168px tall, however many games the catalog holds; it never wraps into columns. Rows touch (no gap, no margin around the list, no rounded corner) and the page scrolls when they do not fit. The three games used most recently (opened or played, newest first) come first under a small-caps **Recently played** heading; the rest follow by name under **All games**. With nothing used yet there are no headings. Names sort as typed, letters and digits only, so R.E.P.O. files under R.

- **Art:** the game's key art edge to edge under a light solid dim layer, with plain text straight on it kept legible by a text shadow and no panel behind. Art is Steam's `library_hero.jpg` read at runtime, never bundled.
- **Left:** the game name, then one line joined with " · ": the loader (installed version, or its name alone before install), the store, a relative last-played time while a last-played profile exists, total playtime, and **Not set up** until the game has a folder and a profile. Nothing on the line repeats what the profile cards show.
- **Profile cards** (installed and set up games): compact cards (name, health badge, relative last-played) in the left column under that line, with the same text shadow. They wrap rather than cut mid-word, at most six, then a **+N more** card that opens the game. Clicking a card opens that profile and does not fire the row's open handler. No Play on a card.
- **Right:** the mod sources as small logo-only badges (Nexus's own mark, the rest from Simple Icons; name in the tooltip and accessible name), with a contained **Play** under them when a profile exists and the game is set up: it opens the game on its last-played profile (else its first) and starts Play through the same Start path as the sidebar button, including SMAPI install and Steam checks. A game available but not set up shows **Set up** (gear icon, Play's size) in its place.
- **Opening:** a live game's tile is always clickable (not set up opens its setup; set up opens its main screen). No tile carries an edge stripe. Art for a tile that cannot open (a game coming later) is desaturated under a darker dim. The row under the pointer or holding keyboard focus grows 72px taller over 450ms, eased in and out, showing more art; the art never shifts sideways or zooms, and rows never dim on hover. Game status reloads when the window regains focus.
- **Letter rail:** once eight or more games sit under **All games**, a 28px rail of initials (**#** for digits) replaces the scrollbar on the right edge (44px wide with 44px letters in roomy mode). Letters share the rail's height up to 48px each, their type growing from 11px to 15px; capped letters are centred and the rail scrolls when they do not fit. Clicking a letter brings its first game to the top and focuses it; the letter of the first alphabetical row in view shows in `--mortar-accent-ink`.
- **Type to find:** typing on the page outside any field, menu or dialog jumps to the first game whose name starts with the letters typed (else the first that contains them), ignoring case, spaces and punctuation ("rep" finds R.E.P.O.). The row is focused, so Enter opens it; a one-second pause starts a new search.
- **No game found:** a footer line **No supported game was found in your launchers.** with **Check launchers…** opening Settings › Launchers.

## Main screen

A left sidebar and a detail pane. Code: `game/MainScreen.tsx`, `game/Sidebar.tsx`, `game/ProfileWorkspace.tsx`.

- **Sidebar:** 220px expanded, 76px as a rail, a `--mortar-nav` surface with a 1px hairline on its right edge. It is one vertical tab list ("Profile sections"): one tab stop on the open section, arrow keys and a gamepad's shoulder buttons move between sections, each tab points at the workspace `tabpanel` by `aria-controls`, and each group is a labelled `role="group"`.
  - **Home**, then small-caps groups of 38px rows (18px Lucide icon, label): **Library** (Mods with a primary badge of available updates, Saves, Config), **Get mods** (Browse), **Health** (Problems with a warning badge of its problem count, Load order, Performance) and **Logs** (Console). Load order, Performance and Console show only when the loader supports them, and a group left empty loses its header. The open row has a filled background and a 3px primary bar at its left edge. Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+8 select sections (Config is Ctrl+6; Home has none), as Settings › Shortcuts lists.
  - **Collapse** (chevron) and **Play** pin to the foot. The rail shows icon-only buttons with a name and tooltip, badges in the top-right corner, an **Expand sidebar** chevron and an icon-only Play. The choice is saved per viewer (Ctrl+B toggles it); below 960px wide the sidebar is always the rail.
  - With no profile open only Play shows. There is no Settings icon here (it displaced Play); Settings stays in the Mortar menu and on Ctrl+,.
- **Play** (`launch/PlayControl.tsx`, `launch/playMenu.ts`): a full-width 64px primary button split with a caret (More play options). The menu is plain items in arrow-key order: a **PLAY WITH** heading and one row per launch preset when the profile has named ones (**Standard** is the profile's own settings; the default ends in **(Default)**), **Set default preset…** (Lucide Star; a radio list that applies on pick), **Play without mods** (Lucide Gamepad2: starts the game with no profile and does not lock mods). When compact the menu opens on right-click only. On Windows, when Steam's launch options run SMAPI, a dialog says "Steam will still start SMAPI" before offering Cancel or **Play without mods**.
- **Detail pane:** the open profile's section. Home alone shows the hero; other sections open with the [Tab header](#tab-header).
- **Compact layout** (below 960px wide; minimum window 768×432): the sidebar is the icon rail with Play at its foot, the hero becomes a one-line header (name and "N mods · N updates · N problems", zero parts left out), the mods search folds into a filter icon, other actions fold into icon buttons, and the mod grid drops to two columns.

### Home

Hero then <a id="home-page"></a>**Home page** (`game/Home.tsx`, `game/Hero.tsx`, `game/HomePanel.tsx`): an action row, then a three-column grid of cards (one column when compact).

- **Hero:** 190px tall bleeding to the pane's edges (one line in the minimum-size layout), with no actions beside the name (they are in the action row below; Rename, cover and the rest live in the profile menus, [Profile management](#profile-management)). The name is large and bold with a soft white glow; the description shows under it as one truncated line with the full text in a tooltip (hidden when compact). A profile from a Nexus collection adds "From the Nexus collection “X” · revision N" ("revision N is out · Review" when newer) and **Unlink**.
  - **Cover:** the cover image at full opacity under a light solid dim layer (`rgba(20,20,24,0.18)`), its alpha fading to transparent over the bottom 20% by an alpha mask so the backdrop shows through; the hero has no background of its own.
  - **Cover source, in order:** an image the user picked, else the Nexus picture of the profile's most-endorsed mod (`endorsement_count` from the mod endpoint), else Steam's hero art for the game (read at runtime from `<Steam>/appcache/librarycache/413150/library_hero.jpg`; never bundled), else a solid tone; never a random image.
- **Actions**, each with a leading icon: **Profile ▾** (This profile, Copies, Review, then **Delete profile…** in the error colour), **Share…**, **Launch ▾** (Rocket icon: **Run with this profile** lists configured tools, one click each, or "No tools yet"; **Manage tools…** adds, edits and removes them; **Shortcuts** for the desktop shortcut and, where Steam can start Mortar, adding the profile to Steam) and, at the right, **<Game> settings**. Everything else (Rename, cover, Check this profile, Export, Match a friend's profile, Send to, collection draft, saved groups, templates) stays in the profile menu.
- **Cards:** **Crash card** (full width, only after a crashed run, hidden by **Dismiss** for that run): cause and evidence from the log, with **Disable it and play** (only when the log names a mod the profile has) and **Find the cause…** (a crash bisect, [architecture.md](architecture.md#launch)). **Notes** (two columns): edited in place, saved 0.8 s after the last keystroke and on **Done**, up to 20,000 characters. **At a glance:** mod, update (opens the update review) and problem counts (opens Problems; absent until first read), created/updated dates and last played. **Changes since last run** (two columns, only when there are changes): first three lines, **Show all**, **History…**. **Saves:** up to four saves newest first (name left, "Year N Season" right, in At a glance's type) and **All saves (N)**; it takes a whole row when it follows At a glance and shares a row with Changes otherwise, so no card sits alone at one column.

### Launching

The launch flow is a sequence of gates and layers (`frontend/src/launch/`):

- **Launch overlay** (`LaunchLayer.tsx`): covers the whole window as an MUI Modal (focus trapped, Esc hides it, the window controls stay clickable through a cut-out) with a spinner, "Launching <game>", SMAPI's first log lines as they arrive, **Open console** and **Hide**, until the game is up or the launch fails (SMAPI exiting without a log fails with its exit code).
- While the game runs, Play becomes a Running status with elapsed time and **Stop game**, and the profile's mod controls lock ("Stop the game to change mods."): switching, removing, installing, dropping archives. While Mortar installs SMAPI itself, Play reads "Installing SMAPI…".
- **Pre-Play dialogs**, each omitted when nothing applies, in this family of rules (the dialog's actions follow [Buttons and dialogs](#buttons-and-dialogs)):

- **Implicit mods** (Show menu and Settings › Appearance › Mods view, `implicitModsView`): **Always shown**, **Nested** (each implicit mod beneath the first mod in its group that requires it, deeper for what that requires, the list row indented with ↳; one nothing in its group requires keeps its place) or **Hidden** (except while a search or Show › Implicit asks).

## Config page

The **Config** tab (Library group after Mods and Saves; Ctrl+6; palette **Switch to Config** and **Configure <mod>**) lists every mod with a config source and edits the chosen one beside the list. Code: `mods/typedConfig/` (`ConfigTab.tsx`, `ConfigPane.tsx`, `Widgets.tsx`, `StringWidgets.tsx`, `keybind.ts`, `convert.ts`). The edit model is typed rows ([architecture.md](architecture.md#profile-operations)).

- **Controls row** under the shared tab header: a growing **Filter N mods** field (name or UniqueID) and **Show** (All, Changed, In-game menu, Waiting for next launch).
- **List (300px, rows at `space.row`):** follows the Mods page's **Group by**, sort, collapse state and headers (the same settings, so a collapsed group stays collapsed in both). One row per mod with a config source (a SMAPI `config.json`, a BepInEx `.cfg` owned through its plugin GUID, a captured in-game menu): icon, name and one chip (**N waiting**, then **Changed**, then **In-game menu**); a disabled mod is dimmed. A last group, **Loader and other**, holds `.cfg` files no mod owns (such as `BepInEx.cfg`), shown while no filter is on. The footer reads "N mods have no config yet; mods create it the first time the game runs." The list is one `configsvc.Mods` call, cached per profile in a store, re-read after a profile change or an edit; the selection is remembered per profile. With nothing to list: **No mod settings yet**, "Mods write their settings the first time the game runs." and **Play**.

- **Files:** the dropdown lists a SMAPI `config.json` or each BepInEx `.cfg` (labelled by its plugin's name) and each other text file the mod keeps in BepInEx's config folder or its package's subfolder there (`.json`, `.yaml`, `.yml`, `.toml`, `.xml`, `.ini`, `.txt`), labelled by file name with the path as tooltip.

A text file no mod owns lists under **Loader and other**.
- **Saving:** each change saves a moment after it is made. A failed write (including one refused because the game runs the profile) shows **Could not save: <reason>** under the row. A search with no match says **No entries match.**

### Rows and value types

A section is a heading above its rows. A row shows the entry's name (the mod's label, else the key split into words at camelCase and number boundaries, with the raw key as tooltip; a label only breaks between words), an info button when the mod gave a description (**About <entry>**), the control and, when the value differs from default, a **Reset to default** icon.

- **Type** comes from the JSON type of the current value, else of the shipped default (a null, or a number or boolean written as text, takes the default's), and a write keeps it: a boolean stays `true`, never `"true"`.
- A **Content Patcher** pack's values are all strings, so its `content.json` `ConfigSchema` decides and a write stays text (`"false"`, several choices comma-joined): `AllowValues` of exactly true and false is a switch, other `AllowValues` a select (multi-select with `AllowMultiple`), none a text field; `Default` is what reset goes back to.
- Control by type: on/off is a switch; a number is a field (a slider when the mod gives a small range); a choice is a select; a colour is a swatch beside its hex field (an invalid value errors) opening a picker leftward into the page; a list is removable chips with **Add an item**; anything else is text. A text value gets the editor its shape calls for, written back in the form the mod already uses:
  - **Keybind:** a key-named entry holding `None`, a SMAPI key or key list (`LeftShift + F5`, alternatives separated by commas) or a Unity InputSystem path (`<Keyboard>/backquote`) keeps its text field and gains **Record** ("Press a key…", takes the next press with Shift, Control and Alt held; Escape cancels) and a clear button. Recording replaces the whole value in its own syntax; clear writes `None`, or nothing for an InputSystem path.
  - **Comma list of words:** two or more short items become chips (Enter adds, × removes), joined with the existing separator.
  - **Number list or vector** (`200, 250, 350`): one small number field per item (an invalid item errors and is not written), **+** appends a copy of the last, × per item while more than one remains, with the value's own separator.
  - **Listed values:** a description that lists its values keeps the suggestion field.
- **In-game menu:** a mod whose Generic Mod Config Menu the bridge captured lists **In-game menu** among its files (the only one when there is no `config.json`, and the details panel then says **Settings from the mod's in-game menu**). Sections are the menu's pages and titles; a choice shows the mod's labels; a colour, image or custom widget reads **Change it in the game**. A changed row says **Applied when the game next starts**, its reset icon is **Keep the game's current value**, and a change the game could not apply shows **The game could not apply the last change: <reason>**. **Presets** is hidden there and **Reset all** asks to discard every waiting change.

A gated source (itch.io without an API key) is disabled with its reason as the tooltip (a capitalised sentence). The search label follows the choice (**Search all mod sites**, **Search GitHub releases**, **Search <source>**).
- **Top mods:** with nothing typed every source lists its top mods, so the tab opens on results; a source's empty-state text (**Find mods to add**) shows only when it has nothing to list.
- **Category pickers** are multi-selects, disabled with **This source has no categories to filter by** for a source without categories.

A sort the source does not offer falls back to best match.

It also holds **Card details** (which stats the cards show).
- **Results:** cards in the Mods grid's spacing, or rows. A card has a thumbnail (read through the local picture cache so a return visit shows every one; a failed one is a plain square), name, author (accent colour), a two-line summary (body ink), a stats line (dim, tabular; ticked under **Show › Card details**), an open-page icon in list rows and the action. More than 20 results page at the foot. Loading shows placeholder cards; no results and a failed search each have an empty state (the latter with **Retry**).

Once pressed the card shows progress in place (**Queued**, **Waiting for Nexus…**, **Downloading N%**, **Installing**, **Needs your input**, **Failed, retry**), then **Added** ("In this profile"). A mod the game's loader is built on shows **Loader**, a companion Mortar installs itself **Built in**. Status chips take the button's width, height and corners.

## Problems tab

A full-height tab showing one section of the profile's problems at a time. Code: `mods/ProblemsTab.tsx`, `problemSection.ts`, `problemGroups.ts`, `ProblemRows.tsx`, `problemFix/`, `problemReport.ts`, `shell/SectionStrip.tsx`; each kind's plain-language explanation is `problemWhy.ts`, backed by [user-guide.md](user-guide.md).

Under the shared tab header, a full-width **section strip** (one MUI tab per non-empty section, label and count chip, the chosen one filled in the primary colour) scrolls sideways when it does not fit, never changes height, and moves with Left and Right (only the chosen tab is in the tab order). It never shows an empty section.

- **Which section shows:** the first that holds something broken (missing requirements, broken mods, damaged files, run errors, failed plugins, plugins shipped twice), else the first with anything. A choice is remembered per profile while Mortar runs, and counts only while its section exists.
- **Section action:** only missing requirements has one, **Add all N** (outlined, Plus icon, off while the queue call is in flight) in a right-aligned 44px row under the tabs; every other section's list starts under the tabs. Rows are dismissed and restored one at a time.
- **Page actions** (tab header): **Find the mod that crashes the game…** (tooltip "Turns mods off in halves and relaunches until the crash stops"; off with the reason as tooltip unless the newest run crashed with no suspected mod: "Start the game once so Mortar can see the crash." with no runs, "No crash to investigate: the last run ended normally." after a clean run; the palette entry follows the same rule), **Asset map** and **Copy report** (plain-text counts by section, each row's sentence, each conflict's **Why?** keys, Dismissed, Cleanup, Redundant, Compatibility and the harmless count; disabled with **No problems to copy.**).
- Under the section body a muted **Checked <relative time>** line (it updates) carries the check timings by family in its tooltip. Loading reads "Checking the mods for problems…"; none reads "No problems found."; checks that could not run offline show the connection warning.

### Sections

In this order, only non-empty ones. "Counted" means it counts toward the tab chip, sidebar badge and Home's At a glance.

| Section | Notes | Counted |
| ------- | ----- | ------- |

| Conflicts | the same mods with the same winner and fix are one row listing every asset | yes, except harmless cosmetic asset conflicts |

| Changed outside Mortar | | yes |
| Duplicates | | yes |
| Deprecated packages | Thunderstore marks them; **Replace with <package>** when one is named | yes |

The count comes from one helper, `problemCount`, feeding the tab chip, sidebar badge, Home's At a glance and cached profile badges, so it is zero only when the tab has nothing to fix. The Mods tab carries no problems banner. While problems load, the chip shows the profile's last known count.

- **Redundant:** mods replaced by an enabled replacement, packs every edit of which later packs overwrite, and C# mods doing the same job (a smaller may be "covered" by a larger). Each same-job group is one row ("A and B do the same job: both change X", or "all N change X") with a **Remove one…** menu naming what each item removes (**Remove A**). Every row has a quiet **Dismiss** before its remove action, for a finding the player judges wrong; dismissed rows stay listed dimmed under a small **Dismissed** label at the foot with **Restore**, and return by themselves when a different set of mods makes the mod redundant.
- **Cleanup rows** read "<name>: Not needed by any enabled mod" or their specific reason ("Made for Earthy Recolour, which is not enabled"), with **Remove**.

### Rows and fixes

- Warnings are amber tiles; information rows (Cleanup included) are neutral tiles with an info icon. A row shows the full wrapped text, severity icon, the Nexus author note when a listed requirement has one, and its fix actions.
- A missing or optional-dependency row links to that UniqueID in the Load order tab (falling back to the dependent). The mod list shows the same problem triangle and tooltip as a card, beside the name so it stays visible when the Status column is narrow or hidden.
- **Fix buttons run in order of weight:** the suggested fix first (a Content Patcher **Set … to …**, or **Make a pack win** when there is none) filled in the warning colour on real conflicts, then **Disable** outlined, then **Dismiss** as a quiet text button last. Dismissed rows keep their normal actions but swap Dismiss for **Restore**. Applying a Content Patcher **Set to** suggestion remembers that field value so the reverse hint stays hidden until the setting changes ([architecture.md](architecture.md#saves)).
- **Conflict rows** expand a **Why?** section listing each pack's clashing patch (source file and index, Action, Target, ToArea/FromArea, When, Priority) and, for image edits, a cropped preview of that pack's overlapping source region loaded when the section opens; it ends with a **Show conflicts** link into the Asset map filtered to that asset with rows open.
- **Asset map** (dialog): the game assets the profile's mods change, by default only those more than one mod changes (a switch shows all), filtered by **Find an asset**, with asset and shared counts and **Changed by N mods** (warning-coloured, with tooltip). An opened asset lists each mod's edit in load order (name linking to the mod, Action, Priority, winner marked); each loser offers **Make win** (outlined, only when loading it later decides the asset) and **Disable** (text). **Show more** loads the next 100.
- A load-order dependency chip names the dependency: an installed copy's name, else the Nexus page the metadata dataset lists for its id (`DependencyNames`, fetched for ids with no row), else the bare id; a missing required one reads "Needs <name> (missing)".

## Load order tab

A read-only list of the open profile's enabled mods in the order the game loader chooses ("The game loader chooses this order. Mortar does not change it.", SMAPI in the line's `title`). SMAPI sorts by dependencies: required and optional dependencies before dependents, content packs after their ContentPackFor framework, otherwise alphabetical (ModResolver). Code: `mods/LoadOrderTab.tsx`, `loadOrderText.ts`; `mortar profile load-order` prints the same list.

- A filter field fills the controls row (the shared `ControlsRow` with a growing `SearchField`, as on Mods, Saves and Console); **Copy load order** (numbered plain text) is in the header's actions slot.
- Each row shows position, name and chips for required dependencies, optional dependencies and dependents; past four required dependencies or two dependents it shows a **Used by N mods** chip expanding the full list. A required dependency the profile lacks is a red chip; a dependency cycle is flagged on its rows; clicking a chip for a listed mod scrolls to that row.
- Loading: placeholder rows in the list's shape ("Reading load order…"). Error: "Could not read load order" with **Retry**. None: "No enabled mods."

## In-between moments

- **Dropping an archive:** while an archive is dragged over the window, a dashed drop zone fills it 12px in from the edges and says where it goes ("Drop to install into Cookie farm") and the supported formats. It shows no file name: WebKitGTK and Wails expose none before the drop.
- **A Nexus link:** on a game's profile screen the link installs into the open profile at once, with a toast naming the mod ("Downloading <mod> into <profile>"). On any other screen a dialog, "Install <mod>?", asks which profile (the open one primary, **Other profile…**, or Ignore); further links queue behind it. A link never brings an open window to the front, so clicking downloads in browser tabs keeps the browser in front; only a window closed to the tray reopens. While the window is minimised, or behind another while a profile question is open, a desktop notification says the same and its Show action raises the window. The mod's name is its Nexus page title ("Nexus mod <id>" when signed out or offline). A refused link (wrong game, another account, expired, malformed) is a toast saying why.
- **Two copies of one mod:** a dialog shows both (source, version, what depends on it) and preselects the newer or Nexus-sourced one, switching the other off rather than deleting it; **Decide later** leaves both as a problem. When exactly one copy is from Nexus, **Keep the Nexus copy** is the recommended contained action; **Keep this one** keeps the selected non-Nexus copy. Code: `mods/DuplicateDialog.tsx`.
- **Loading:** a pane that waits on data shows the shared `SkeletonRows` in the shape of the content (announced by a label), never an empty pane.

## Console tab

SMAPI's (or the loader's) log as it is written, monospace, in columns: level bar, time, level, mod, message; warnings and errors get a tinted row. Code: `console/ConsoleTab.tsx`, `VirtualLog.tsx`, `LogActions.tsx`, `LevelToggles.tsx`, `RunsPicker.tsx`, `consoleLinks.ts`, `smapiUpdateNotes.ts`.

- **Filter row:** a growing search box; non-wrapping level toggles for Info, Warn, Error and Alert, each with a compact line count (6.2K); a **More levels** menu with Trace and Debug checkboxes and counts; a mod picker whose choices show as removable chips; Clear filters; the **Runs** picker; and at the right icon toggles (tooltip, aria-label, `aria-pressed`) for timestamps (Clock) and following new lines (ArrowDownToLine).
- **Log actions** sit in the tab header's page actions, not the filter row, as icon buttons with tooltip and aria-label: an error group (jump to first, previous, next error), **Copy log**, **Save log…** (native save dialog, default name `SMAPI-<profile>-<date>.txt`; the raw SMAPI log when this profile owns it, else the Console's lines), **Search all runs…** (searches every stored run and opens a match at its line), **Clear** (Copy and Clear off with no lines), then **Share log** (LifeBuoy): shows the size, states it becomes public at a link, asks before uploading to smapi.io/log, copies the link and offers Open.
- **Runs picker** (button reads This session or the run shown): This session, or one of the profile's last 20 recorded launches (outcome Ran, Crashed or Failed, with time). A past run loads its stored log read-only, and Save log and Share log use it. "No recorded runs yet" before the first.
- **Links:** in visible rows only, an exact installed mod name or `UniqueID` is an underlined link in the row's own colour that selects the mod, and a path under the profile's `mods/` folder or the game folder opens its folder.
- **Update alert tags:** each line of SMAPI's **You can update** alert ends with a **Mortar:** tag saying how Mortar treats that version (from `UpdatesResult.Held` and the update list): already installed (only the manifest's number is stale), skipped, pinned, updates ignored, source skipped, prerelease, unofficial build, in Mortar's updates, or not downloadable here; the tooltip explains it.
- **Before the first launch** a line says the console fills when the game runs. When SMAPI's own log says it is incompatible with the game's version, the console offers to reinstall SMAPI.
- **Input line** at the bottom (monospace, prompt `>`): hidden until this profile is running and This session is shown. Enter sends the command to the running game through the Mortar SMAPI Bridge and echoes it into the log as a Mortar line prefixed `>`; output arrives with SMAPI's own lines and Follow is switched back on. Up and Down browse the last 100 commands of that game (memory only). A failed send toasts.

## Performance tab

One scrolling page with no inner switch; its only toolbar is the tab header, and its empty state (a timer icon, **No startup measured yet**, copy that says to play the profile) is centred like other tabs'. Code: `console/PerformanceTab.tsx`, `StartupPanel.tsx`, `StartupSections.tsx`, `StartupTable.tsx`, `PerformancePanel.tsx`, `PerformanceComparison.tsx`, `useBridgePerf.ts`.

- **Page actions:** the run picker (last 10 launches, when more than one) and **Measure next launch** (off while a measurement is requested, also when none exists yet). Once requested, an info banner above it, **The next launch will be measured**, offers **Play now** (off while the game runs) and **Cancel**.
- Below, in order:
  - **What to do:** up to three one-line findings from the selected run, each with one button: the mod that dominates the start (3 s or more) with **Show packs** / **Show mod** (expands its row); a slowdown of 1 s or more against the launch before with **Compare** (scrolls to the table, sorts by change, adds a Change column); SMAPI's own mod loading when it takes 5 s and a tenth of the start with **Show slowest mods**. With none it says nothing stands out.
  - **Where the time goes:** one stacked bar across the start, a coloured segment per phase (SMAPI loads mods, Mods start, Game content, First updates, Title intro) sized by time, with a legend (colour, name, time) below; hovering explains the phase.
  - **Slowest mods:** a sortable table of Mod, Total (longest first by default) and Why, one short phrase from the mod's own timings ("slow UpdateTicked 19.2 s", "assets and packs 16.9 s"). A row expands to Entry, Slowest event, Assets and packs and, after a measured launch, Sampled, plus a framework's content packs by time (25 shown, then a count). Clicking a name shows it in Mods; Mortar's bridge row carries a **Mortar** label. Mods under 50 ms in both fold into **N other mods**; **Game and SMAPI** holds the unattributed rest.
  - **In game:** a collapsible section, closed until the profile has saved reports. Reports are saved per profile and compared in the tab: before/now average milliseconds, signed change and percentage, with new and gone mods called out.

### In-game measurement

Start measuring and Show report are off while the game is not running this profile or a past run is shown. Before measuring the empty state is a gauge icon, **See which mods slow the game**, copy to start the game and measure while playing, and **Start measuring**; afterwards **Measuring**, **Show report**, **Copy report**. With no rows: **No performance data was returned.**, or **Start measuring to view a report.** when the log held no summary. The source depends on the loader's `perf` capability:

- **Console fallback** (no companion measurement): Start measuring sends SMAPI's `performance enable`, Show report sends `performance summary` and fills a sortable table (Mod or event, Average ms, Peak ms, Calls).
- **SMAPI with its bridge** (the same panel as BepInEx's; it replaces the console report): Start measuring asks the bridge for `perf start`, timing each mod's handlers of the per-frame events; no measured launch is needed. Show report reads `perf`; the table adds **95th ms** and **% of frame** when rows carry them. Above it: the frame line (fps, average, 95th and 99th percentile and worst frame ms, managed heap, garbage collections), a **Baseline** line (the mods' timed code in ms of the average frame, and the game and loader alone, which is the frame less the mods' timed code, not a launch with no mods) and a line saying a row is a mod's update, render and one-second handlers summed per frame, with Harmony patches and work outside events left in the baseline.
- **BepInEx:** the bridge measures only on a launch measured from Startup, so the empty state says to use **Measure next launch** first, and says the launch was not measured when Start measuring finds it was not. Start measuring asks for `perf start`; Show report reads `perf` as data and saves it, rows named by package (its plugins added up). The same frame, baseline and row-meaning lines appear; a row counts Harmony prefixes, postfixes and finalizers and the plugin's own Update, LateUpdate and FixedUpdate, exclusive of nested timed calls, not transpiled code or other threads.

## Sync offers

With a sync folder set (Settings › Mods and profiles), a change another computer made to a profile is announced once by an info toast, **<profile> was changed on <machine>** (or **… and here** when both sides changed it), with **Review**. **Review** opens **Changes from your other machines**, a dialog with one block per waiting change and **Later** to close it:

- The block's title, and for a change both sides made, **Both sides changed it, so Mortar will not merge them. Pick one side.**
- **Apply** (contained; **Use theirs** for a change both sides made) makes this computer's profile match the other's, creating the profile when it exists only there. **Keep mine** (not for a profile new to this computer) keeps this computer's profile and writes it over the other revision. **Show diff** lists **Adds <mod>** and **Removes <mod>** rows, or **Only settings and options differ.**
- Mod files are never part of a sync; the mods an apply adds download from their sources.

## Browser extension

The MV3 extension (https://github.com/Rethunk-Tech/mortar-browser-extension) on `www.nexusmods.com`. When the two sides do not share a native-messaging protocol, its popup, button and filter section say **Update Mortar: it is too old for this browser extension** or **Update the browser extension: it is too old for this Mortar**. Native-host requests: [architecture.md](architecture.md#nexus-mods).

- **Popup:** label **On Nexus pages**, a select **Off (do not mark Nexus pages)** / **Highlight** / **Hide mods in profile**, stored in `chrome.storage.local` as `mode`. Status under it: **Mortar isn't installed. Install Mortar and open it once to connect this browser.** (**Open Mortar** is hidden then), **Mortar isn't running** (**Open Mortar** becomes the primary button), **Open a profile in Mortar**, **Browser extension connection is off in Mortar**, **Mortar could not read this profile**, or **Connected to Mortar · N Nexus mods in \<profile\>**. Then **Open Mortar** and **Check now** (disabled while it runs) buttons, and, only while connected, an **Updates** list (**Updates in \<profile\>**) linking each mod with its installed and latest version, or **No updates**.
- **Listings, search, and collections:** in Highlight, mod tiles (`data-e2eid="mod-tile"` and Nexus's other tile attributes) whose Nexus id is in the open profile get a green outline and an **In profile** badge, or an amber **Update available** badge when Mortar knows of an update; Hide removes those tiles from the page; Off draws nothing, including the Mortar filter section. The app's **Browser extension connection: Off** has the same effect everywhere (marks, panels, labels, updates); a connection problem shows as a muted line in the Mortar filter section. Collection pages (`/games/<game>/collections/<slug>` with tiles linking to `/<game>/mods/<id>`) use the same marks. Mortar's UI (filter section, mod-page button, collection panel) appears only on games the native host lists as supported (Stardew Valley today); other games' pages are left alone. In Hide mode the installed row is disabled with its reason shown under it, and counts the removed tiles. A disabled row always shows its reason as text, and the download dialog is followed only when Mortar is installed and not Off.
- **Listing filters:** a Mortar section at the top of Nexus's filter sidebar (`#filters-panel`, built from Nexus's own classes) with three rows that dim and desaturate tiles in place, so the listing keeps its length; hovering a dimmed tile brings it back up. All rows are saved per game in the extension's storage.
  - **Gray out mods in profile:** the tiles of mods in the profile last open for that game.
  - **Gray out obsolete:** tiles whose title, or summary where the word speaks for the mod (its start, "This mod/file…", or a heading line), says obsolete, deprecated or depreciated: the same words and rule as the Problems check's author-marked mods (`authorStatusWord`; the extension's tests pin the same words). It needs no connection to Mortar.
  - **Gray out broken:** mods SMAPI's compatibility list marks broken for the version last played ([architecture.md](architecture.md#nexus-mods)); it needs Mortar installed but not running.
- **Mod page:** a panel under the title: **In \<profile\>: v\<version\>** or **Not in \<profile\>**, **Also in:** other profile names, **Nexus has a newer version** when the page version is newer, **Update available in N of your profiles** when the installed version in those profiles is older than the page's latest file (or Mortar's cached Nexus details mark an update), **Required by N mods in \<profile\>** (names on hover) when enabled mods in the active profile require it, **Pinned** / a skipped version or source when those are set on the entry, and **Open in Mortar** (`mortar://<game>/mod/<id>`). Off removes the panel. The panel is read-only.
- **Files tab:** one badge per file that any of your profiles has installed, listing those profile names (open profile first, highlighted).
